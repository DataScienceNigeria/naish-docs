The Hub does not start from scratch. National use cases arrive with **existing
datasets, prior corpora, third-party models, and inherited licensing terms** that
must be assessed, cleaned, and governed before any of them touch the compute
pipeline. This page provides practical guidance on how teams should handle
dataset licensing, pre-processing, bias, and multilingual readiness - turning
raw assets into pipeline-ready, legally sound, representative data.

## Dataset licensing, provenance, and access

Every dataset entering the Hub must pass a licensing and provenance review.
On a shared national resource serving health, agriculture, and education, an
unresolved licence term can block an entire workload - or worse, expose the Hub
to legal liability after deployment.

### What teams must establish

- **Licence type and permissions** - identify the exact licence (Creative
  Commons variant, government open-data licence, proprietary agreement, or
  bespoke research licence) and confirm it permits the intended use: training,
  fine-tuning, inference, redistribution, or commercial deployment. Many
  open-source corpora permit research but restrict commercial use, a distinction
  that matters when the Hub serves production national services.
- **Consent constraints** - for any data involving personal information -
  especially health records, voice recordings, and educational assessments -
  verify that the original consent covers AI training and model deployment, not
  only the study or service the data was collected for. Alignment with the
  **Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA)** is mandatory, not optional. Health and
  financial datasets require explicit, informed consent review.
- **Provenance and chain of custody** - document where the dataset originated,
  who collected it, how it was transmitted to the Hub, and whether any
  intermediate processing (anonymisation, translation, re-labelling) altered
  its composition. If provenance is unclear, the dataset is not ready.
- **Access conditions and restrictions** - some datasets are available only
  under data-use agreements, embargo periods, or institutional access controls.
  Record these constraints in the workload metadata so scheduling and
  retention policies respect them. Data that must be deleted after a training
  window must not linger on the 7.6 TB scratch or MinIO object store.

### Practical checklist

| Check                    | Question to answer                                          | Blocking if unresolved                  |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Licence on file          | Is the full licence text stored alongside the dataset?      | Yes                                     |
| Use-case match           | Does the licence permit training, fine-tuning, and serving? | Yes                                     |
| NDPA alignment           | Has a data-protection assessment been completed?            | Yes - for personal data                 |
| Consent scope            | Does original consent cover AI model development?           | Yes - for health, voice, education data |
| Provenance log           | Is the collection-to-Hub chain documented?                  | Yes                                     |
| Retention terms          | Are deletion or expiry obligations recorded?                | Yes - if time-bound                     |
| Third-party dependencies | Are upstream dataset licences compatible?                   | Yes - for derived or composite datasets |

## Pre-processing standards

Raw data is not pipeline-ready data. The Hub requires teams to follow consistent
pre-processing standards so that datasets arriving from different institutions,
projects, and languages can be staged, combined, and versioned without ad-hoc
guesswork.

### Cleaning

- **Remove duplicates** - exact and near-duplicate records inflate training
  sets and skew evaluation metrics. Deduplication should happen before any
  split.
- **Handle missing values explicitly** - document whether missing fields are
  dropped, imputed, or flagged. Silent imputation introduces hidden bias.
- **Strip artefacts** - encoding errors, HTML tags, OCR noise, and
  transcription timestamps that are not part of the target signal must be
  removed or normalised. For voice data, strip non-speech segments (silence,
  background noise, interviewer prompts) unless they are labelled training
  targets.
- **Validate schema** - every dataset should conform to a declared schema
  (column names, types, units, expected ranges) before it enters the pipeline.
  Schema mismatches between institutions are one of the most common causes of
  silent training failures.
- **Detect and redact personal data** - run automated PII detection over every
  dataset before staging: names, phone numbers, NINs, BVNs, addresses, dates of
  birth, facility identifiers, and free-text fields where identifiers leak.
  Redact, hash, or pseudonymise according to what the use case actually needs,
  and record which strategy was applied per field. Free-text clinical notes and
  transcribed speech are the highest-risk surfaces - identifiers appear inside
  the content, not only in the metadata columns. This is the concrete step that
  makes the consent and NDPA position above enforceable rather than declared.

### Annotation

- **Annotation guidelines** - publish written guidelines before annotation
  begins, not after. Guidelines should define label categories, edge cases,
  and inter-annotator agreement targets.
- **Multi-annotator review** - for safety-critical use cases (clinical,
  educational assessment), require at least two independent annotators per
  sample and measure inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa or equivalent).
  Single-annotator labels are acceptable only for low-risk, high-volume tasks
  with spot-check audit.
- **Language-aware annotation** - annotators must be fluent speakers of the
  target language and dialect. Annotation by non-speakers - even with
  translation tools - introduces systematic labelling error that is difficult
  to detect downstream.

### Normalisation

- **Text normalisation** - standardise character encoding (UTF-8), casing
  rules, punctuation, and diacritics. For Nigerian languages with tonal
  marking (Yoruba, Igbo), define whether tone marks are preserved, stripped,
  or normalised to a canonical form, and apply that rule consistently.
- **Audio normalisation** - resample to a consistent rate (16 kHz is the
  Whisper standard), normalise amplitude, and segment into uniform-length
  clips or sentence-aligned utterances.
- **Numeric and unit normalisation** - standardise currency (Naira), dates,
  measurements, and geographic references to canonical formats so models do
  not learn spurious distinctions between "N5,000" and "5000 Naira".

### Synthetic, augmented, and machine-translated data

Low-resource languages tempt teams into manufacturing data. Manufactured data is
permitted on the Hub, but never silently.

- **Label it** - every synthetic, augmented, or machine-translated record must
  carry a provenance flag distinguishing it from human-produced data. A dataset
  that mixes the two without labelling cannot be audited and does not pass the
  readiness gate.
- **Keep it out of test and evaluation splits** - synthetic and
  machine-translated records may appear in training data. They must **never**
  appear in validation or test splits. Evaluating a Nigerian-language model on
  machine-translated English benchmarks measures the translator, not the model,
  and reliably overstates performance.
- **Machine translation is not a substitute for in-language data** - translating
  an English corpus into Hausa or Igbo reproduces English syntax, idiom, and
  cultural framing in local-language surface forms. Use it, if at all, as a
  bootstrap for training volume - never as the basis for evaluation, and never
  as a reason to skip native-speaker data collection.
- **Record the generator** - for synthetic data, document which model or rule
  set produced it, with what prompt or configuration, and at what date. Synthetic
  data generated by a model that was itself trained on the target test set is a
  leakage path.
- **Cap the ratio** - declare the proportion of synthetic to human data in the
  data card. Where synthetic data dominates a language or class, say so
  explicitly: it is a known limitation of the resulting model, not an
  implementation detail.

### Data splits

- **Train / validation / test splits** - define splits before any model
  training begins and freeze them. Use **80/10/10 for large datasets**, where
  10% still yields enough samples per language and demographic group to measure
  disaggregated performance; use **70/15/15 for smaller datasets**, where the
  larger held-out fractions are needed to make per-group metrics meaningful. The
  deciding question is not the ratio but whether the smallest group you intend
  to report on has enough test samples to produce a stable number. The key
  requirement is that splits are **stratified** by language, region, and
  demographic group so that no split is unrepresentative.
- **Speaker and source independence** - for voice and text data, ensure that
  the same speaker, author, or source institution does not appear in both
  train and test splits. Leakage across splits inflates reported accuracy and
  hides real-world failure modes.
- **Temporal splits where applicable** - for time-series data (health records,
  agricultural yield), split by time period rather than randomly to avoid
  look-ahead bias.
- **Version and publish splits** - register splits in the Hub's experiment
  tracking (MLflow) so that every evaluation references an immutable,
  reproducible partition.

### Dataset versioning

Registering splits is not enough. The question the Hub must be able to answer
about any deployed model is **"which data trained this?"** - and that requires
the dataset itself to be versioned, not only its partitions.

- **Assign a dataset version ID** to every dataset that enters the pipeline, and
  increment it on any change to content, schema, pre-processing, or
  pre-processing parameters. A re-run with a different cleaning rule produces a
  new version, not an update to the old one.
- **Make versions immutable** - a version, once registered and used in training,
  is never modified in place. Corrections create a successor version with a
  documented reason for the change.
- **Record the version alongside the split** in MLflow, so every experiment
  references both the dataset version and the partition within it.
- **Store a content hash** for each version so that a dataset on the object store
  can be verified against the version it claims to be.
- **Keep the lineage** - where a dataset derives from an upstream corpus
  (AfricanVoices, a partner dataset, a previous version), record the parent
  version and the transformation applied. Composite datasets record every parent.

## Training guidelines

Data readiness ends where training begins, but the same reproducibility and
governance obligations carry through. A model whose training run cannot be
reconstructed cannot be audited, reproduced, or safely retrained after an
incident.

### Reproducibility

- **Pin the environment** - record the container image digest, framework
  versions, CUDA and driver versions, and the node configuration the run
  executed on. A tag is not a pin; a digest is.
- **Set and record random seeds** for data ordering, initialisation, and
  augmentation. Where full determinism is impractical on GPU, say so and record
  which sources of non-determinism remain.
- **Log the full configuration** - hyperparameters, optimiser, schedule, batch
  size, gradient accumulation, precision (fp16, bf16), and any distributed
  training topology - to MLflow, automatically rather than by hand.
- **Reference the dataset version and split** registered above. A run that does
  not name both is not reproducible and should not be promoted.
- **Record the base model and checkpoint** where the run is a fine-tune,
  including its source, licence, and revision.

### Running and checkpointing

- **Checkpoint regularly** and retain the checkpoints an evaluation or rollback
  might need - not only the final weights. On a shared national resource, a run
  that must restart from zero after a pre-emption wastes capacity other teams
  were queued for.
- **Define stopping criteria in advance** - target metric, patience, and a
  maximum compute budget. Runs without a declared stopping condition are the
  main source of idle GPU consumption on a shared node.
- **Declare a compute budget per run** at submission, sized from a short pilot
  run rather than estimated. Teams should expect to justify budgets that exceed
  their pilot's implied cost.
- **Monitor for divergence and silent failure** - loss plateaus, NaNs, and
  collapsing validation metrics should abort a run, not consume its full budget.
- **Evaluate on the frozen test split only at the end.** Repeatedly consulting
  the test split during development converts it into a validation set and
  invalidates the reported result.

### Evaluation and promotion

- **Report disaggregated metrics** by language, dialect, gender, region, and any
  other axis the representation audit identified. Aggregate accuracy alone does
  not satisfy the [Responsible AI](/docs/capabilities/responsible-ai) gate.
- **Evaluate in-language with native speakers** for generative and conversational
  outputs, per the multilingual guidance below.
- **Test code-switched and dialectal inputs** as a standard part of evaluation,
  not as an optional extra pass.
- **Register the trained model** in MLflow with its dataset version, split,
  configuration, environment digest, and evaluation results attached. A model
  without this record does not proceed to deployment review.

## Bias identification, mitigation, and responsible multilingual development

Bias in data becomes bias in models. On a national resource serving 200 million
people across hundreds of languages and dialects, unaddressed bias does not just
degrade accuracy - it excludes communities. Bias mitigation is therefore a
pre-processing and governance obligation, not a post-hoc audit.

### Identifying bias

- **Representation audit** - before training, measure the demographic
  composition of the dataset: language distribution, geographic coverage,
  gender balance, age range, urban/rural split. Compare against the target
  population. If Hausa speakers are 30% of the target users but 5% of the
  training data, the model will underserve them.
- **Label distribution audit** - check for class imbalance and systematic
  labelling patterns that correlate with annotator demographics or source
  institutions.
- **Historical and selection bias** - datasets collected from urban hospitals,
  university campuses, or digital platforms systematically under-represent
  rural, low-literacy, and offline populations. Document these gaps explicitly
  rather than treating the dataset as nationally representative by default.
- **Measurement bias** - different collection instruments, microphone quality,
  or transcription standards across sites introduce variation that models may
  learn as signal rather than noise.

### Mitigating bias

- **Targeted data collection** - where audits reveal under-representation,
  prioritise supplementary data collection for the missing groups before
  training, rather than relying on algorithmic fixes after the fact.
  [AfricanVoices](/docs/projects-archive/archive/african-voices) is an example
  of this approach: deliberately curating speech across Hausa, Igbo, Nigerian
  Pidgin, and Yoruba with demographic metadata.
- **Stratified sampling** - when full balance is not achievable, use stratified
  sampling or oversampling of minority groups to reduce skew during training.
  Document the sampling strategy and its rationale.
- **Disaggregated evaluation** - do not report aggregate accuracy alone. Break
  evaluation metrics down by language, dialect, gender, region, and any other
  relevant demographic axis. A model that scores 92% overall but 68% on Igbo
  inputs is not ready for national deployment.
- **Bias documentation** - publish a data card for every dataset entering the
  pipeline, documenting known biases, limitations, and the populations for which
  the data is and is not representative. The required fields are set out below.

### Data card requirements

A data card is the dataset's public record. It is mandatory for every dataset
entering the pipeline, and it is what a reviewer reads when deciding whether a
model built on that dataset can be deployed. So that cards are comparable across
teams and institutions, every card carries the same fields:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Dataset name and version** | Name plus the immutable version ID and content hash |
| **Owner and custodian** | Originating institution, and who is accountable for it on the Hub |
| **Licence** | Exact licence, permitted uses, and any redistribution limits |
| **Consent basis** | What consent was obtained, for what purpose, and whether it covers AI training and deployment |
| **Provenance** | Collection method, dates, instruments, and the chain from collection to Hub ingestion |
| **Composition** | Record count, size, modalities, schema, and label set |
| **Language and dialect coverage** | Languages, dialects, and the distribution across them |
| **Demographic coverage** | Geography, gender, age, urban/rural, and any other relevant axis, with distributions |
| **Pre-processing applied** | Cleaning, normalisation, annotation, and PII redaction steps, with parameters |
| **Synthetic content** | Proportion and provenance of synthetic, augmented, or machine-translated records |
| **Splits** | Split definition, ratios, stratification, and independence guarantees |
| **Known biases and gaps** | Under-represented groups, selection and measurement bias, and populations the data does **not** represent |
| **Appropriate uses** | Use cases the dataset supports |
| **Inappropriate uses** | Use cases it must not be used for, stated explicitly |
| **Retention and deletion** | Retention period, deletion obligations, and expiry date if time-bound |
| **Contact and review date** | Who to ask, and when the card was last reviewed |

Two rules make the card useful rather than ceremonial: **state the gaps
explicitly** - a card that lists only what the dataset covers is a marketing
document - and **update the card with the dataset version**, since a card that
describes a superseded version is worse than no card at all.

### Responsible multilingual AI development

Building AI that works across Nigerian languages requires more than translating
English prompts. It demands linguistic and cultural grounding at every layer.

- **Language-first design** - define the target languages and dialects at
  project inception, not as an afterthought. Language choice shapes data
  collection, annotation, model selection, and evaluation - retrofitting
  multilingual support onto an English-only pipeline is expensive and
  produces weaker results.
- **Use local-language base models where available** - the **N-ATLaS-LLM 8B**
  and **AfricanVoices** corpora exist precisely to avoid starting from
  English-only foundations. Fine-tuning a Nigerian-language model on
  domain-specific data is more effective than fine-tuning a purely English
  model with translated examples.
- **Evaluate in-language** - test models with native-speaker evaluators in each
  target language, not with back-translated English benchmarks. Fluency,
  cultural appropriateness, and factual accuracy must be judged by speakers
  of the language.
- **Handle code-switching** - Nigerian users frequently switch between English
  and local languages within a single utterance or message. Models must be
  tested on code-switched inputs, not only monolingual samples.
- **Respect dialectal variation** - Hausa spoken in Kano differs from Hausa
  spoken in Sokoto; Yoruba in Lagos differs from Yoruba in Ibadan. Where
  dialectal data exists, preserve it rather than collapsing to a single
  standard form. Where it does not, document the gap.

## Alignment to the Hub pipeline and governance model

These standards are not standalone - they connect directly to the Hub's existing
capability stack and governance framework.

- **Data readiness gate** - datasets that have not completed licensing review,
  pre-processing, and bias audit do not pass the
  [Data Readiness](/docs/capabilities/data-readiness) assessment and are not
  admitted to the compute pipeline. This is a hard gate, not a recommendation.
- **Feasibility dependency** - a use case cannot be judged
  [technically feasible](/docs/capabilities/feasibility-assessment) if its data
  assets are unlicensed, unprocessed, or unrepresentative. Data readiness is a
  prerequisite for feasibility sign-off.
- **Governance framework integration** - licensing terms, consent constraints,
  and retention obligations feed into the
  [Governance &amp; Risk](/docs/capabilities/governance-risk) workload governance
  framework. Access controls enforced via Keycloak and data residency on MinIO
  ensure that governed data stays governed through the pipeline.
- **GenAIGov alignment** - teams can use the
  [GenAIGov](/docs/projects-archive/archive/genaigov) STANDARD framework to
  self-assess governance maturity across the Data, Transparency, and
  Accountability dimensions before requesting Hub access.
- **Responsible AI gate** - bias audit results and disaggregated evaluation
  metrics are inputs to the
  [Responsible AI](/docs/capabilities/responsible-ai) assessment. A model that
  has not been evaluated for fairness across languages and demographics does
  not pass the pre-deployment safety gate.
- **Experiment tracking** - all pre-processing steps, split definitions, and
  bias audit results should be logged in MLflow so they are reproducible and
  auditable, consistent with the
  [Model Lifecycle](/docs/capabilities/model-testing) workflow.

## Practical guidelines for teams

The following guidelines summarise what teams working with Nigerian and
multilingual datasets should do before, during, and after data preparation.

### Before starting

1. Obtain and file the full licence text for every dataset.
2. Complete an NDPA data-protection assessment for any personal data.
3. Verify consent scope covers AI training and deployment.
4. Document provenance from original collection to Hub ingestion.
5. Define target languages, dialects, and demographic coverage requirements.

### During pre-processing

6. Deduplicate, clean, and validate against a declared schema.
7. Run PII detection and apply the declared redaction or pseudonymisation
   strategy, including over free-text and transcribed fields.
8. Normalise text encoding (UTF-8), audio sample rate (16 kHz), and units.
9. Annotate with published guidelines, multi-annotator review for high-stakes
   tasks, and language-fluent annotators.
10. Flag any synthetic, augmented, or machine-translated records, and keep them
    out of validation and test splits.
11. Run a representation audit: language, region, gender, age, urban/rural.
12. Create stratified, speaker-independent train/validation/test splits.
13. Assign an immutable dataset version ID with a content hash, and record its
    lineage.
14. Register the dataset version, splits, and pre-processing metadata in MLflow.

### Before submitting to the pipeline

15. Publish a data card with all required fields, stating coverage gaps and
    inappropriate uses explicitly.
16. Confirm all licensing, consent, and retention terms are recorded in workload
    metadata.
17. Verify that disaggregated evaluation baselines exist for each target language
    and demographic group.
18. Submit dataset for [Data Readiness](/docs/capabilities/data-readiness) review.

### During and after training

19. Pin the environment by image digest and record seeds, configuration, and
    node topology in MLflow.
20. Reference the dataset version and frozen split in every run.
21. Declare a compute budget and stopping criteria before submitting the run.
22. Evaluate on the test split once, report disaggregated metrics, and test
    code-switched and dialectal inputs.
23. Register the trained model with its full data, configuration, and evaluation
    lineage attached.

Teams that follow these steps will clear the Hub's data-readiness and governance
gates without rework - and build models that serve the populations they are meant
to serve.
