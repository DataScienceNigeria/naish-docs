Credential verification asks a different question from patient identification.
The record is not in the national register - it is in a credentialing platform,
like the school records behind
[DiCert MCP](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dicert-mcp). What NIMC adds is the
link between that record and a person: **this certificate is real, and the
person presenting it is the person it was issued to.**

Those are two separate checks, and conflating them is the common design error.

This is a reference workflow applying the
[NIMC Identity API](/docs/integration/integration-toolkit/nimc-identity-api) to
the [education template](/docs/templates/education).

## Two checks, not one

| Check | Question | Source | Failure means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Credential authenticity** | Does this certificate exist as issued? | Credentialing platform | Forged or altered document |
| **Identity binding** | Is the presenter the person it was issued to? | NIMC eNVS | Impersonation with a genuine certificate |

A solution that checks only authenticity confirms the paper and not the person.
One that checks only identity confirms the person and not their claim. Most real
fraud lives in the gap: a genuine certificate presented by someone else.

## Assurance decision

| Action | Harm if wrong | Level | Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A learner views their own results | Minor privacy leak | **Low** | Platform account login |
| A parent views a child's results | Family privacy | **Low-Medium** | Platform account plus relationship on the school record |
| A school or employer verifies a presented certificate | An unqualified person admitted or hired | **Medium** | `searchByDocumentNumber` or `searchByNIN` |
| Award a place, licence, or professional registration | Irreversible; harms a displaced candidate | **High** | `verifyFingerWithData` against the claimed NIN |

## Workflow: verifying a presented credential

1. **Check the credential first.** Query the credentialing platform for the
   certificate number. If it does not exist as issued, stop - no identity call is
   needed, and querying the national register about a person whose document is
   already invalid is an unnecessary lookup.
2. **Obtain and record consent** from the presenter for an identity check, with
   the purpose stated: confirming that this credential belongs to them.
3. **Collect the asserted identity** - the NIN, or the document number recorded
   on the credential.
4. **Call the 1:1 method**: `searchByNIN` where a NIN is asserted, or
   `searchByDocumentNumber` where the credential carries a registered document
   number.
5. **Compare against the credential record**: names and date of birth returned by
   eNVS against the names and date of birth the credentialing platform holds.
6. **Escalate to biometric confirmation** where the decision is irreversible -
   `verifyFingerWithData` against the asserted NIN - so that the person present
   is confirmed, not merely the paperwork.
7. **Retain only the verification outcome**, a timestamp, and the credential
   reference. The demographic record is not stored against the application; it
   was consulted, not collected.
8. **Record the audit event** with identifiers hashed.

## Comparing names is harder than it looks

The most common false rejection is not fraud - it is orthography. A name recorded
at NIMC registration may differ from the same name on a school certificate issued
years earlier by:

- **Diacritics and tone marks** - present in one record, stripped in the other.
- **Name order** - surname first on one document, last on the other.
- **Middle and other names** - `middlename`, `othername`, `maidenname` in
  `demoData`, often absent or abbreviated on a certificate.
- **Previous names** - `psurname`, `pfirstname`, `pmiddlename` exist precisely
  because names change, most often on marriage.
- **Transliteration variants** of the same name across Nigerian languages.

So: **normalise before comparing** - case, diacritics, punctuation, and name
order - and compare against previous names as well as current ones. Treat a
partial match as a **review case**, never an automatic rejection and never an
automatic acceptance. Where the decision matters, a name mismatch escalates to
biometric confirmation or to human review, with the discrepancy recorded.

## Failure paths

### Credential not found

The certificate does not exist as issued. Report it as unverifiable, record the
attempt, and follow the awarding body's escalation route. No identity call.

### Identity does not match the credential

Do not conclude fraud. Re-check the normalisation, check previous names, then
route to human review with the specific discrepancy shown - "date of birth
differs", not "verification failed". A registry correction is a more common
explanation than impersonation.

### eNVS unreachable

Fail closed on the identity check. The credential-authenticity result still
stands and can be reported on its own, clearly labelled as
*credential verified, identity not verified*. Two checks means one can be
reported without the other - provided the label says so.

## What the AI layer sees

This is the case where the
[DiCert MCP](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dicert-mcp) pattern is most directly
applicable: AI clients - a parent's assistant checking results, a teacher
reviewing class performance, an administrator pulling verification statistics -
call purpose-built tools over the credentialing platform, never the identity
service.

| Agent receives | Agent never receives |
| --- | --- |
| Credential records via bounded platform tools | The eNVS connection or any of its methods |
| "Identity verified" / "not verified" / "under review" | NIN, `demoData`, photo, signature |
| Aggregate verification statistics | Per-person identity records |
| An internal learner ID | The identity behind it |

The identity check runs in the integration layer and reaches the agent as a
status. A model must never be able to trigger a national identity lookup, and
must never hold one in context.

## Pre-launch checklist

1. Both checks specified separately, with the two-result reporting format
   defined.
2. Credential check ordered first, so no identity lookup runs against an invalid
   document.
3. Name normalisation implemented and tested against real Nigerian name shapes -
   diacritics, order variation, previous names.
4. Partial-match threshold agreed with the awarding body, and the review route
   staffed.
5. Consent wording reviewed, including for minors, where a guardian's consent
   applies.
6. Retention confirmed: outcome and reference only, no demographic record stored.
7. NIN confirmed absent from logs, URLs, analytics, and any learner-facing view.
8. Agent tool surface reviewed: no path from the model to eNVS.
