---
title: "Use Case: Farmer Enrolment"
description: A reference workflow for low-assurance enrolment over phone and chat channels - why a phone number is not an identity, when an identity call is justified at all, and how to avoid excluding the farmers a service exists to reach.
---

An agricultural advisory service reaches farmers over WhatsApp, SMS, USSD, and
IVR. Most of what it does - answering a question about pest control in Hausa,
pushing a planting-window alert - needs no identity at all. The temptation is to
verify everyone at enrolment anyway, because the API is there.

Resist it. Every verification step placed in front of a smallholder farmer on a
feature phone is a point where enrolment fails. The design question is not "how
do we verify?" but **"which actions actually need an identity, and how little
will do?"**

This is a reference workflow applying the
[NIMC Identity API](/docs/integration/integration-toolkit/nimc-identity-api) to
the [agriculture template](/docs/templates/agriculture).

## Assurance decision

| Action | Harm if wrong | Level | Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Answer an agronomic question | None | **None** | No identity call |
| Send seasonal advisory content | None | **None** | No identity call |
| Remember a farmer's crop, location, and history | Wrong personalisation; mild privacy leak | **Low** | Channel identifier only |
| Link to a cooperative or extension officer's roster | Wrong person joins a group | **Low-Medium** | Channel identifier; `searchByDemoPhone` only if the roster requires it |
| Disburse a subsidy, input voucher, or payment | Financial loss; fraud | **High** | `verifyFingerWithData`, or facial authentication with liveness, against a claimed NIN |

Most of the service sits in the top two rows. An identity call is justified at
the bottom row, where money moves - and the biometric burden is proportionate
there because the consequence is real and the interaction is usually in person,
at an agro-dealer or extension point with a device present.

**Fingerprint is the wrong default for this population.** Farming wears
fingerprints down, and a smallholder who has spent forty years working the land
may simply have no readable print left. Facial authentication with liveness,
available through
[NINAuth and NIMC verification partners](/docs/integration/integration-toolkit/nimc-identity-api),
needs only the camera on the phone already in the room. Where a programme offers
one biometric modality only, choose it with this population in mind; where it can
offer both, the farmer should not be the one who discovers the limitation.

## The default: no identity call at all

Advisory content is not personal data. A farmer asking when to plant maize in
Kaduna needs an answer, not an account. Design the service so that:

- **Advisory works anonymously**, on first contact, with no enrolment step.
- **Personalisation is opt-in** and earns its cost - the farmer volunteers crop,
  location, and farm size because the advice visibly improves.
- **Identity verification appears only at the step that requires it**, with the
  reason stated in the farmer's own language.

A service that demands a NIN before answering a question will be abandoned at
that screen by the users it was funded to reach.

## Low assurance: the channel identifier

For remembering a farmer between conversations, the **phone number the message
arrived on** is the identifier. It requires no API call, no consent to query the
national register, and no personal data beyond what the channel already carries.

`searchByDemoPhone` resolves a registered phone number to an identity in the
national register. It is a **1:N identification** call and returns a full
`demoData` record. For personalising farming advice, that is disproportionate:
it queries national identity infrastructure to solve a problem the channel
already solved. Use it only where a roster or programme genuinely requires the
registered identity behind a number, and record why at the governance gate.

## High assurance: the payment or entitlement step

When a subsidy, voucher, or input disbursement is at stake:

1. **State the purpose plainly**, in the farmer's language: what is being
   verified, why, and by whom. Consent is recorded with a timestamp and purpose.
2. **The farmer asserts their NIN** - spoken to an extension officer, entered at
   an agro-dealer terminal, or captured at a cooperative office.
3. **Capture the biometric the setting supports** - a fingerprint at a known
   position on an attached reader, or a facial capture with liveness on the
   device present.
4. **Call the 1:1 check** - `verifyFingerWithData` with the NIN, the base64
   fingerprint, and the position code; or the facial verification the programme
   is onboarded for. Either confirms the claim the farmer has made; neither
   searches the register for whoever matches.
5. **Retain only what the disbursement record needs**: a verification outcome, a
   timestamp, and the encrypted NIN as reference. Not the photo, not the
   next-of-kin block, not the residence block.
6. **Discard the biometric** immediately after the call.
7. **Record the audit event** with identifiers hashed.

## Failure paths

### Biometric does not match

Farming is manual work, and manual work wears fingerprints down. Expect a
materially higher fingerprint no-match rate in this population than in an office
setting - and design for it as the normal case, not the exception.

The flow: retry once; try a second finger position; then **switch to facial
authentication** where the programme supports it, since a farmer with unreadable
prints usually has a perfectly verifiable face. Only when every available
modality has failed does the flow route to **documented manual verification** by
a named extension officer or cooperative official, recorded against the
disbursement. A farmer who cannot produce a usable fingerprint is not a fraud
case, and the workflow must not treat them as one.

### No NIN, or a NIN that does not match

Enrolment in the national register is not universal, and records can be stale.
The service must have a documented route for an unenrolled farmer: a lower-value
entitlement tier, a referral to enrolment, or officer attestation - agreed with
the programme owner before launch.

### eNVS or the network is unreachable

Rural connectivity is the norm, not the edge case. Verification **fails closed**
- the disbursement is not released on an assumed pass - but the farmer is not
sent home without a path. Queue the verification, issue a receipt or reference,
and complete on reconnection. Whether value can be released before verification
completes is the programme owner's risk decision, taken and written down in
advance.

### Advisory continues regardless

Whatever happens at the payment step, the advisory service keeps working. A
verification failure must never degrade the free, anonymous advice the farmer
came for.

## What the AI layer sees

The advisory agent - a multilingual assistant over WhatsApp, SMS, or IVR - has no
identity access at all:

| Agent receives | Agent never receives |
| --- | --- |
| An opaque conversation ID | The phone number as an identifier of record |
| Crop, location, farm size the farmer volunteered | NIN or `demoData` |
| "Eligible for programme X" as a flag | The verification record, fingerprint, or face capture |
| Language preference | Any national-register field |

Verification and disbursement sit outside the conversational layer. The agent
learns only that an entitlement applies, through a bounded tool following the
[DiCert MCP](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dicert-mcp) pattern - never who the
farmer is in the national register.

## Pre-launch checklist

1. Documented justification for every step that calls eNVS - and confirmation
   that advisory does not.
2. Confirmation that the channel identifier gates nothing of financial
   consequence.
3. Consent wording translated into each supported language and tested for
   comprehension over voice as well as text.
4. Biometric failure path tested for each modality offered, including the
   switch between them and the officer-attested fallback.
5. Route for unenrolled farmers agreed with the programme owner.
6. Offline and queued-verification behaviour tested on a real rural connection
   profile, not on office wifi.
7. Field retention list signed off; full `demoData` discarded at the boundary.
8. Agent tool surface reviewed: no identity fields reachable by the model.
