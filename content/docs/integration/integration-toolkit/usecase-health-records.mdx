---
title: "Use Case: Patient Identity in Health Records"
description: A reference workflow for resolving and confirming a patient identity at the point of care - assurance sizing, the consent-first call sequence, offline and no-match paths, and the fields a health record may retain.
---

A health-record solution must answer one question before it does anything else:
**is this the person whose record I am about to open?** Get it wrong and a
clinician reads someone else's history, or writes a diagnosis into the wrong
file. Get it wrong in the other direction - demand a fingerprint from a patient
whose hands are damaged, at a clinic with no working reader - and the patient is
turned away.

This is a reference workflow for that decision, applying the
[NIMC Identity API](/docs/integration/integration-toolkit/nimc-identity-api) to
the [health-records template](/docs/templates/health-records).

## Assurance decision

| Action | Harm if wrong | Level | Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Show general health information | None | None | No identity call |
| Register a new patient | Duplicate record created | Medium | `searchByNIN` |
| Open an existing clinical record | Wrong history read; wrong care given | **High** | `verifyFingerWithData` |
| Write a diagnosis, prescription, or result | Harm to two patients at once | **High** | `verifyFingerWithData` |

Reading and writing clinical data is irreversible in effect - a wrong entry
propagates into later care - so it sits at high assurance. Registration sits a
step lower: a duplicate record is a data-quality problem, correctable, not a
clinical one.

## Workflow: first registration

1. **Explain and record consent.** The patient is told the clinic will verify
   their identity against the national register, for the purpose of creating a
   health record. Consent is recorded with a timestamp, the purpose, and the
   staff member who obtained it.
2. **Collect the NIN** from the patient and validate its format locally.
3. **Call `searchByNIN`** with the cached token.
4. **On match**, extract only the fields the record needs (see below) and create
   the patient record with an **internal record ID** as its key. The NIN is
   stored once, encrypted, as a verification reference - never as the primary
   key, and never displayed in the clinical UI.
5. **Enrol the biometric reference** for subsequent visits: capture a fingerprint
   and confirm it against the NIN with `verifyFingerWithData`, so that the
   patient's later visits need no NIN re-entry.
6. **Record the audit event**: purpose, outcome, staff member, timestamp,
   correlation ID. Identifiers hashed.

## Workflow: returning patient at the point of care

1. **Confirm consent** for this visit's lookup - consent is per lookup and
   purpose, not captured once at registration and reused indefinitely.
2. **Capture the fingerprint** at the enrolled position.
3. **Call `verifyFingerWithData`** with the NIN held against the record, the
   base64 fingerprint, and the position code.
4. **On a confirmed match**, open the record and let care proceed.
5. **Discard the biometric** from memory immediately. It is never written to
   disk, never queued, never logged.
6. **Record the audit event.**

The fingerprint is compared against the identity the record already claims - a
1:1 check. The solution never searches the national register for whoever matches
the print in front of it.

## Fields a health record may retain

| Field | Retain | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `surname`, `firstname`, `middlename` | Yes | Patient identification on the record |
| `birthdate` | Yes | Clinically necessary - dosing, risk, screening intervals |
| `gender` | Yes | Clinically necessary |
| `nin` | Yes, encrypted, not as a key | Verification reference for later visits |
| `telephoneno` | Yes, if the service contacts patients | Follow-up and results |
| `photo`, `signature` | **No** | Not clinically necessary; high-sensitivity biometric data |
| Next-of-kin block | Only if the service uses emergency contacts, and captured directly from the patient in preference | Available in `demoData`, rarely justified by clinical need |
| `religion`, `profession`, `educationallevel`, `emplymentstatus`, `maritalstatus` | **No** | Not clinically necessary; retaining them is exactly the over-collection the NDPA prohibits |
| Residence and origin blocks | Only the fields a catchment or follow-up workflow actually uses | Not "the whole address block because it was returned" |

The response arrives as a full `demoData` record. Minimisation happens at the
integration boundary - the fields above are extracted, the rest is discarded
before anything reaches clinical storage.

## Failure paths

### No match on NIN

Causes: mistyped NIN, a name recorded differently at registration, genuine
non-enrolment. **A patient is never refused care because the register did not
match.**

The flow: re-enter the NIN once; if it still does not match, create the record as
**unverified**, flagged for later verification, and proceed with care. An
unverified record is usable clinically and visibly marked as unverified, so that
it is not later mistaken for a confirmed identity.

### Fingerprint does not match

Causes: worn or damaged fingerprints (common among manual workers, the elderly,
and patients with certain conditions), a poor reader, a wet or dirty finger.

The flow: retry once, then offer an alternative finger if another position was
enrolled. On continued failure, fall back to **staff-attested identification** -
a named clinician confirms the patient by other means and the record notes who
attested and why. Two consecutive biometric failures is a service-design signal,
not a fraud signal.

### eNVS unreachable

VPN down, upstream outage, clinic offline. Verification **fails closed** - the
solution never assumes a pass - but **care does not stop**.

The degraded mode, agreed in advance with the health authority: the clinician
proceeds using the record they can identify by other means, entries are written
to a queue marked *pending verification*, and the queue is reconciled when the
service returns. Whether this is acceptable, and for which actions, is a clinical
governance decision recorded before launch.

## What the AI layer sees

Where the solution has an AI assistant - summarising history, suggesting
follow-up questions, drafting patient instructions - it operates on the clinical
record, never on the identity record:

| Agent receives | Agent never receives |
| --- | --- |
| Internal record ID | NIN |
| Clinical history and observations | `demoData`, photo, signature |
| Age, derived from `birthdate` | Next-of-kin details |
| "Identity verified" / "unverified" flag | The fingerprint or its template |

The agent cannot call eNVS. Verification happens in the solution's integration
layer and reaches the agent only as a flag, through a bounded tool following the
[DiCert MCP](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dicert-mcp) pattern.

## Pre-launch checklist

1. Assurance levels agreed per action with the health authority, in writing.
2. Consent wording and storage reviewed for clinical context and NDPA
   compliance.
3. Field retention list signed off; the discard step tested against a full
   `demoData` response.
4. No-match path tested end to end, including the unverified-record route.
5. Biometric failure path tested, including the staff-attested fallback.
6. Degraded mode agreed with clinical governance and the reconciliation queue
   tested.
7. NIN confirmed absent from logs, URLs, analytics, and the clinical UI.
8. Agent tool surface reviewed: no identity fields reachable by the model.
