title: Integration Guide & Resource Catalogue
description: Catalogue of existing ecosystem assets, datasets, tools, frameworks, and governance resources relevant to Hub use cases - with technical prerequisites, access pathways, constraints, and practical integration routes into solution design, build, and deployment.
---

NAISH does not start from zero. A large share of what a national use case needs: speech corpora, governance frameworks, DPI connectors, credentialing middleware,
low-code delivery platforms etc., already exists inside the Nigerian AI ecosystem,
most of it built or funded through the same programmes that produced the Hub.
The failure mode is not absence of assets; it is teams rebuilding an asset they
did not know existed, or adopting one without checking whether its licence,
access terms, or maturity fit the job.

This page is the **integration guide and resource catalogue**: what exists, what
it takes to use it, where it does and does not fit, and how it enters a Hub
solution.

## How to use this catalogue

1. **Search before you build.** At use-case scoping, check this catalogue for an
   asset that already covers part of the problem. Reuse is the default; a new
   build needs a reason.
2. **Read the fit-for-purpose notes, not only the description.** Several assets
   are production-grade in one dimension and prototype-grade in another.
3. **Confirm prerequisites and access before committing to a design.** Access
   negotiation - API keys, data-use agreements, partner sign-off - is often the
   long pole, not the engineering.
4. **Record the asset in workload metadata.** Every asset a solution depends on
   is a dependency the Hub must track for governance, licensing, and risk.


## Catalogue structure

Every catalogued asset is described against the same six fields, so that teams
can compare like with like:

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Asset & owner** | What it is, who owns and maintains it |
| **Type** | Dataset, model, platform, framework, API, or toolkit |
| **Technical prerequisites** | What a team must have or stand up to use it |
| **Access pathway** | How a team obtains access - open, API key, agreement, partner request |
| **Constraints** | Licence, consent, coverage, maturity, and operational limits |
| **Fit-for-purpose** | Where it works well, where it does not |

## Ecosystem asset catalogue

### Datasets and corpora

#### AfricanVoices

- **Owner** - Data Science Nigeria (DSN), Gates-funded.
  [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/african-voices)
- **Type** - Speech corpus (~1,900 hours) across Hausa, Igbo, Nigerian Pidgin,
  and Yoruba, with demographic metadata.
- **Technical prerequisites** - object storage for audio at scale, an ASR
  training or fine-tuning pipeline (Whisper-family or equivalent), and GPU
  allocation on the node. Audio should be normalised to 16 kHz before training,
  per the [data readiness standards](/docs/capabilities/data-readiness).
- **Access pathway** - request through DSN as data custodian; access is governed
  by the corpus data-use terms and recorded in workload metadata.
- **Constraints** - consent and licensing terms attach to the recordings and
  must be reviewed against the intended use (training vs redistribution).
  Dialect coverage is uneven across the four languages; treat the corpus as
  strong but not nationally uniform.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - strong for ASR and voice-interface use cases in the four
  covered languages (Oral Reading Fluency, voice agricultural advisory, health
  coaching over voice). Not a substitute for domain-specific clinical or
  agricultural terminology data, which must be supplemented.

#### Masakhane datasets and benchmarks

- **Owner** - Masakhane, grassroots pan-African NLP collective.
  [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/masakhane)
- **Type** - Open datasets, models, and benchmarks for African languages,
  including Nigerian and health-relevant resources.
- **Technical prerequisites** - standard NLP tooling; most resources are
  distributed through open repositories and Hugging Face.
- **Access pathway** - open access, per-resource licence.
- **Constraints** - licences vary per dataset; verify each individually rather
  than assuming the collective's general openness applies to every artefact.
  Resource maturity ranges from research prototype to well-maintained benchmark.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - best used as **evaluation and benchmarking** resources
  for Nigerian-language models, and as a supplementary text corpus. Benchmarks
  here support the in-language evaluation required by the
  [Responsible AI](/docs/capabilities/responsible-ai) gate.

#### N-ATLaS-LLM 8B

- **Owner** - Nigerian-language open-source model, referenced across Hub use
  cases. See [Voice &amp; Local-Language Datasets](/docs/capabilities/voice-language-datasets).
- **Type** - Open-weight base model tuned for Nigerian languages.
- **Technical prerequisites** - GPU allocation sized for 8B fine-tuning or
  inference; standard serving stack on the node.
- **Access pathway** - open weights.
- **Constraints** - base-model quality varies by language and domain; requires
  domain fine-tuning for clinical, agricultural, or legal content.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - the default starting point for Nigerian-language
  generation tasks. Preferred over fine-tuning an English-only model with
  translated examples.

### Governance frameworks

#### GenAIGov - STANDARD framework

- **Owner** - DSN, Gates-funded; live at
  [genaigov.ai](https://www.genaigov.ai/en).
  [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/genaigov)
- **Type** - Self-assessment platform scoring AI governance maturity across
  eight dimensions (Systems, Transparency, Algorithms, Norms, Deployment,
  Accountability, Risk, Data).
- **Technical prerequisites** - none; it is a web application.
- **Access pathway** - open, self-service.
- **Constraints** - it is a **readiness assessment, not a certification**. A
  high score does not substitute for the Hub's own
  [governance and risk](/docs/capabilities/governance-risk) review or for NDPA
  compliance.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - use it **before** requesting Hub access, as a structured
  self-check. Its Data, Transparency, and Accountability dimensions map closely
  onto the Hub's data-readiness and responsible-AI gates, so the exported report
  is useful evidence in a use-case submission.

#### Securing AI-Powered Solutions principles

- **Owner** - DSN.
  [Reference](/docs/projects-archive/archive/securing-ai-systems)
- **Type** - Security principles for AI systems - bounded tool surfaces, gated
  access, normalised read views over raw data.
- **Technical prerequisites** - none; applied at design time.
- **Access pathway** - open documentation.
- **Constraints** - principles, not a compliance checklist; they must be
  translated into concrete controls per solution.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - mandatory reading for any solution that exposes national
  data to an AI client. Pairs directly with the sandbox integration toolkit and
  [API playbooks](/docs/integration/sandbox-toolkit).

### Platforms and middleware

#### DiCert MCP - AI-DPI middleware pattern

- **Owner** - DSN. [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dicert-mcp)
- **Type** - Model Context Protocol (MCP) server exposing DPI data as governed,
  callable tools for AI clients.
- **Technical prerequisites** - an MCP-capable AI client; HTTP/SSE connectivity;
  API-key management.
- **Access pathway** - reference implementation runs over DiCert, Nigeria's
  school credentialing platform; access via DSN.
- **Constraints** - the reference deployment is scoped to credentialing data.
  Reusing the pattern over a different DPI requires building the tool surface
  for that platform.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - the **reference architecture** for any Hub solution where
  an AI agent must act on DPI data. Adopt the pattern - bounded read tools,
  normalised views, API-key gating - rather than letting a model query a DPI
  database directly.

#### AI-DPI Sandbox stack

- **Owner** - CcHub, DSN, and DIAL, Gates-funded.
  [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/ai-dpi-sandbox)
- **Type** - Managed, API-based sandbox unifying AI services, a Nigerian DPI
  stack (NIN, BVN, Liveness), open-source tools (DHIS2, PostgreSQL), APIs
  (Maps), apps (QR, notifications), and network channels (SMS, USSD, IVR,
  voice).
- **Technical prerequisites** - sandbox credentials; an application capable of
  calling REST APIs.
- **Access pathway** - via the sandbox programme and its successor arrangements
  under NAISH.
- **Constraints** - sandbox credentials and data are for **testing**, not
  production identity verification; production DPI access requires separate
  authorisation from the relevant platform owner.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - the standard environment for validating DPI integration
  patterns before requesting production access. See the
  [Sandbox Integration Toolkit and API Playbooks](/docs/integration/sandbox-toolkit)
  for the concrete workflows.

#### CommCare and Open Chat Studio (Dimagi)

- **Owner** - Dimagi. [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/dimagi)
- **Type** - Open-source, offline-first platforms for frontline health and
  development data collection and chat-based services.
- **Technical prerequisites** - hosted or self-hosted deployment; standard web
  and mobile delivery.
- **Access pathway** - open source, with commercial hosting available.
- **Constraints** - platform-shaped: adopting it means adopting its data model
  and deployment conventions.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - strong for frontline data collection and offline-first
  health workflows, where rebuilding equivalent field-tested tooling would be
  wasteful.

#### Gooey.AI

- **Owner** - Gooey.AI. [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/gooey)
- **Type** - Low-code platform for multilingual AI copilots over WhatsApp and
  voice, with proven frontline health and agriculture deployments.
- **Technical prerequisites** - channel credentials (WhatsApp Business API or
  equivalent); content and knowledge base preparation.
- **Access pathway** - commercial platform with open components.
- **Constraints** - third-party hosting implies a data-residency and
  governance review before any Nigerian personal data flows through it.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - fast route to a multilingual conversational pilot. For
  production national services, assess against the Hub's data-residency
  requirements first.

#### GovLLMiner / AIKano

- **Owner** - DSN. [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/govllminer)
- **Type** - Offline-first, governance-tuned LLM piloted for Kano State,
  extracting evidence from unstructured government data on local GPU.
- **Technical prerequisites** - local GPU; document ingestion pipeline.
- **Access pathway** - via DSN.
- **Constraints** - piloted at state scale; national-scale behaviour unproven.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - the closest existing precedent for government-document
  question answering on sovereign infrastructure.

#### EdoDiDA chatbot on DHIS2

- **Owner** - DSN, Gates-funded.
  [Project page](/docs/projects-archive/archive/edodida)
- **Type** - Natural-language chatbot over DHIS2 producing plain-language
  insight and recommendations from government health data.
- **Technical prerequisites** - DHIS2 instance and API access.
- **Access pathway** - via DSN and the relevant state health authority.
- **Constraints** - tied to the DHIS2 data model and to state-level data-sharing
  agreements.
- **Fit-for-purpose** - the reference pattern for conversational analytics over
  an existing national health information system.

## Technical prerequisites: the common baseline

Regardless of which asset a team adopts, the Hub expects the same baseline
before integration work begins:

- **A declared use case** that has passed
  [feasibility assessment](/docs/capabilities/feasibility-assessment).
- **Licensing and consent review completed** for every dataset the asset carries
  or produces, per the data licensing standards in the
  [Dataset Readiness and Training guidelines](/docs/integration/dataset-readiness).
- **An identity and access plan** - which team members and services hold which
  credentials, and how those are issued and revoked.
- **A data-residency decision** - whether the asset processes Nigerian personal
  data outside the Hub, and whether that is permitted.
- **A dependency record** in workload metadata, so that a change or outage in an
  upstream asset is traceable to the solutions that depend on it.

## Integration pathways

An asset enters a Hub solution through one of four pathways. Choosing the right
one early prevents rework.

### 1. Direct data integration

The asset is a dataset or corpus that is ingested, pre-processed, and staged for
training or evaluation.

- Run the full data-readiness workflow: licensing review, cleaning,
  normalisation, bias audit, stratified splits.
- Register splits and pre-processing metadata in MLflow so evaluations are
  reproducible.
- Store derived datasets on Hub object storage with the retention terms the
  source licence requires.

### 2. Model reuse and fine-tuning

The asset is a model or set of weights adapted to the use case.

- Confirm the model licence permits fine-tuning and the intended deployment
  (research, public service, commercial).
- Record the base model, checkpoint, and training data lineage in MLflow.
- Evaluate in-language and disaggregated by demographic group before the
  [Responsible AI](/docs/capabilities/responsible-ai) gate.

### 3. API and service integration

The asset is a running platform or API the solution calls at runtime - a DPI
service, an MCP middleware layer, a messaging channel.

- Validate the integration in the sandbox before requesting production access.
- Apply the bounded-tool-surface pattern: purpose-built read tools, no open
  query access, API-key or token gating.
- Define timeout, retry, and failure behaviour - a national service cannot fail
  open when an upstream DPI is unavailable.

### 4. Pattern and framework adoption

The asset is a design pattern, governance framework, or playbook rather than
running code.

- Document explicitly which parts were adopted and which were adapted.
- Where a framework produces a score or report (GenAIGov), attach the output to
  the use-case submission as evidence.

## Discoverability and reuse

A catalogue nobody can search is a document, not an asset register. The Hub
maintains discoverability through:

- **Structured entries** - every asset carries the same six fields, so entries
  are comparable and filterable by type, language coverage, domain, and access
  model.
- **Use-case tagging** - assets are tagged against the Hub's identified use
  cases (agricultural advisory, digital health coaching, health-record
  digitisation, oral reading fluency, government document intelligence) so a
  team scoping a use case sees relevant assets immediately.
- **Maturity labelling** - each asset is marked production, pilot, or research
  prototype, so teams can judge the risk of depending on it.
- **Dependency visibility** - because assets are recorded in workload metadata,
  the Hub can answer the reverse question: which solutions depend on this asset
  if it changes, degrades, or loses its licence.
- **Contribution route** - solution teams submit newly identified or newly built
  assets to the Data Governance &amp; Integration Lead for cataloguing. Assets
  built on the Hub are expected to be catalogued, not kept private by default.

## Publication and governance of the catalogue

- The catalogue is **published to the Technical Working Group and solution
  teams** as part of the Hub's operating documentation.
- Entries are **reviewed when an asset's licence, access terms, or maturity
  changes**, and at minimum on the Hub's regular documentation review cycle.
- **Governance resources in the catalogue are not optional reading.** Where an
  entry is a framework or security principle set, solutions in the relevant
  domain are expected to demonstrate alignment at their governance gate.
- Disputes about whether an asset is fit for a given use case are resolved by
  the TWG, not by the adopting team alone.

## Summary checklist

Before integrating any ecosystem asset:

1. Confirm the asset is catalogued; if not, submit it for cataloguing.
2. Read its constraints and fit-for-purpose notes, not just its description.
3. Verify licence and consent terms cover the intended use.
4. Confirm the access pathway and begin access negotiation early.
5. Choose the integration pathway (data, model, API, or pattern) explicitly.
6. Validate in the sandbox before touching production DPI or production data.
7. Record the asset and its version in workload metadata as a tracked
   dependency.
8. Attach any governance outputs (GenAIGov report, bias audit, data card) to the
   use-case submission.
